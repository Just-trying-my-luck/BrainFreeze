# CLOTHING & DRESS

## EDWARDIAN CIVILIANS

### WORKING-CLASS MEN

#### EVERYDAY CLOTHING

Flat cap, sack coat, waistcoat, braces, detachable collar, necktie, cords or serge trousers, and hobnailed boots.

#### COMMON ALTERNATIVE HEADWEAR

Bowler hats were common among clerks and other working men.

### WORKING-CLASS WOMEN

#### EVERYDAY CLOTHING

Shirtwaist (blouse), tailored skirt, apron, shawl, corset, and boots.

#### SUNDAY BEST

Sunday best could include a hat with simple trim.

### MIDDLE AND UPPER-CLASS MEN

#### DAY CLOTHING

Morning coat worn during the day.

#### FORMAL CLOTHING

Frock coat used for formal occasions.

#### LOUNGE SUIT

The lounge suit was becoming increasingly common during the Edwardian period.

#### ACCESSORIES

Depending on occasion, men might wear a bowler, Homburg, or topper, along with gloves and a walking stick.

### MIDDLE AND UPPER-CLASS WOMEN

#### CORSETS AND SILHOUETTE

The S-bend corset was characteristic of the early 1900s.

#### SKIRTS

Gored skirts were common. By approximately 1910–1912, women's fashion moved toward a slimmer line, including hobble-skirt influences.

#### COLLARS AND HATS

High collars and elaborate picture hats were characteristic of Edwardian fashion.

#### AT HOME

Tea gowns were worn at home.

### EDWARDIAN OCCASIONS

#### SCHOOL

Caps and blazers were common in some schools. Some girls' schools used gym slips.

Many elementary-school pupils did not have a complete uniform and instead wore pinafores or smocks.

#### CHURCH

For Church of England services, "Sunday best" was expected. Hats and gloves were customary.

Men removed their hats when inside the church.

## MILITARY

### WORLD WAR I

#### BRITISH ARMY OTHER RANKS

The standard uniform from approximately 1914–1918 was the 1902 Service Dress, consisting of a khaki wool tunic and trousers.

#### HEADGEAR

The Brodie steel helmet was introduced in 1915.

#### FOOTWEAR

Ammunition boots were worn with puttees.

#### WEB EQUIPMENT

1908 Pattern Web Equipment was used to carry ammunition and other equipment.

#### FIELD KIT

Typical equipment included a greatcoat, small pack or large pack, water bottle, entrenching tool, and bayonet.

#### PRIMARY RIFLE

The SMLE rifle was the standard British service rifle.

### WORLD WAR II

#### BRITISH ARMY OTHER RANKS

Battledress was used in 1937 and 1940 patterns.

#### WEB EQUIPMENT

1937 Pattern Web Equipment was standard issue.

#### HEADGEAR

Mk II and Mk III helmets were used.

#### UNIFORM

The Battledress blouse and trousers were worn with ammunition boots.

#### FIELD KIT

Typical equipment included a greatcoat, gas mask (respirator), small pack or valise, water bottle, and entrenching tool.

#### WEAPONS

Weapons varied according to the soldier's arm or role.

## WORLD WAR II CIVILIANS

### UTILITY CLOTHING

#### CC41

Utility Clothing standardized materials and cuts to conserve fabric during wartime.

### MAKE DO AND MEND

#### CLOTHING REPAIR

"Make Do and Mend" became an important part of civilian wartime culture, encouraging people to repair, alter, and reuse clothing rather than replace it.

### CIVILIAN GAS MASKS

#### ISSUE

Civilian gas masks were issued early in the war.

### EVENING WEAR

#### SIMPLIFIED FASHION

Evening wear became simpler during the war as materials and resources were restricted.

## FASHION CHANGES

### WOMEN

#### EDWARDIAN PERIOD

High collars and corseted silhouettes characterized early-20th-century women's fashion.

#### 1920S

The silhouette shifted toward a lower, drop-waist style.

#### 1930S

Bias-cut clothing and a more elegant, streamlined silhouette became prominent.

#### WORLD WAR II

Utility clothing emphasized practicality. Hems became shorter and decorative features such as excessive pleating were reduced.

### MEN

#### EDWARDIAN PERIOD

Frock coats and morning coats were important elements of men's formal clothing.

#### EARLY 20TH CENTURY

The lounge suit increasingly became the standard everyday suit.

#### WORLD WAR II

Wartime austerity reduced decorative extras and emphasized practical clothing.
